Einstein, Tagore and the Nature of Reality

Two great minds, two conceptions of reality, and the books they inspired.

The conversation

Is the world independent of us?

On 14 July 1930, Rabindranath Tagore met Albert Einstein at Einstein's house in Caputh, near Berlin, and they talked at length about the nature of reality. Is the world a unity that depends on humanity, or a reality independent of the human factor?

Einstein held that reality is independent of mind. Tagore took the opposite view. Yet both claimed to be realists, with fundamentally different conceptions of what that means. Can the two be harmonised at a deeper level? Can Wittgenstein, perhaps, serve as a bridge?

Edited by Partha Ghose

The 2016 volume

Einstein, Tagore and the Nature of Reality (Routledge, 2016) explores the two conceptions of reality from the perspectives of theories of space-time, quantum theory, general philosophy of science, cognitive science and mathematics.

  • Embodied Cognition and the Constructivist View of Reality (Partha Ghose)
  • Appendices: the Einstein–Tagore conversations on the nature of reality, and on youth, causality and music
